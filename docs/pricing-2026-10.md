> **Correction (2026-10-21):** the Gate is **60 ft** across, not 50 ft (James

A priced bill of materials for the V2 upgrade, so the team knows how much to raise.
Prices were checked on **2026-10-21** by read-only browsing; nothing was ordered,
carted or quoted. Specs come from manufacturer datasheets or spec pages, linked
per line. Prices come from the manufacturer's store or a distributor. Retailer
listings are used only where no distributor price was reachable, and are marked.

All prices are USD before sales tax and shipping. Quantities assume 16 segments
plus spares as stated. Ring geometry is in feet; everything else is metric and °C.

**Confidence key**

| Mark         | Meaning                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Quoted**   | Price seen today on the manufacturer's store or a distributor page, at the URL given                                                                  |
| **Quoted\*** | Price seen today, but only in a search-result snippet or an aggregator (Findchips) because the page itself blocked the fetch. Recheck before ordering |
| **Estimate** | A range from comparable parts, or no US price found                                                                                                   |
| **Assumed**  | Depends on an unknown V1 fact; the assumption is named                                                                                                |

UniFi prices: store.ui.com shows two numbers, a base price and a "Surcharge incl."
price. This file budgets the **surcharge-inclusive** price and gives the base in
brackets.

**The joint.** Each segment carries two cable runs: from its node box (entering
through a gland, RJ45 crimped inside) to each end of the segment, ending in an
etherCON TOP plug. A joint is two plugs and one inline coupler: $61.23 per joint,
$980 for 16. Box-mounted receptacles (NE8FDX-P6-W, $23.79, [Markertek](https://www.markertek.com/product/ne8fdx-p6-w/neutrik-ne8fdx-p6-w-d-shape-cat6a-panel-connector-shielded-feedthrough-rubber-sealing-ip65-when-mated-nickel))
are an alternative at $69.82 per joint. Neutrik only states IP65 for that receptacle
mated with NE8MX6, not with the TOP plug.

**Flexible-cable option (in the high total).** UISP Cable Pro has solid
conductors, which suit a run fixed along the truss but not repeated flexing at the
joints. The flexible alternative is Shattuc tactical CAT6A SCDX6AT-RO-00, $1.99/ft
× 270 ft = $537 ([Markertek](https://markertek.com/product/scdx6at-ro-00/shattuc-scdx6at-ro-00-heavy-duty-indoor-outdoor-category-6a-24awg-stranded-data-tactical-cable-per-foot)):
stranded, foil + braid, UL sunlight-resistant, 75 °C, OD 8.3 mm. Its specs come
from the retailer only. That OD needs the NE8MX6 plug ($23.54, [Digi-Key](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/result?keywords=NE8MX6),
OD 7.0–9.5 mm, −40 to 70 °C, [neutrik.com](https://www.neutrik.com/en/product/ne8mx6)),
36 × $23.54 = $847. Belden 7953A DataTuff is $3.44–4.00/ft
([Markertek](https://www.markertek.com/product/bl-7953a-bk/belden-7953a-cmx-msha-datatuff-category-6-outdoor-cable-4-bonded-pair-600v-awm-23-awg-solid-copper-per-foot-black)),
too dear for the gain.

**Amphenol RJField** (IP67/IP68, −40 to 85 °C) has no inline female part, so it
only works box-mounted, at about $310 per joint (Estimate,
[Findchips](https://www.findchips.com/search/RJFTV6A7A1N)).

## 2. Power

### Load estimate (Assumed)

V1's chipset, voltage and density are **Unknown** (`docs/v1-hardware.md`). Assumed:
**12 V WS2815, 60 LED/m**, 4 spokes per segment (1,512 px), 90 % supply
efficiency, about 60 W per segment for the switch and PixLite. Three figures bracket
it:

- **High: 0.3 W/LED (18 W/m).** BTF-Lighting's WS2815 page says "Theoretical
  Power: 0.3W/LED, 60LEDs/m" ([BTF](https://www.btf-lighting.com/products/s-shape-ws2815-rgb-led-strip)).
- **Mid: 14.4 W/m (0.24 W/px).** Superlightingled's WS2815 reel listing
  ([listing](https://www.superlightingled.com/dc12v-ws2815-upgraded-ws2812b-5m-300-leds-individually-addressable-digital-strip-lights-dual-signal-wires-waterproof-dream-color-programmable-5050-rgb-flexible-led-ribbon-light-p-2133.html)).
- **Low: 8 W/m (0.133 W/px).** BTF's IP68 WS2815 page says "Power Max 8W/M"
  ([BTF IP68](https://www.btf-lighting.com/products/ip68-ws2815-addressable-rgb-led-strip)).

doubles the pixel load (up to about 134 A at 120 V).

## Assumptions

1. **Pixels: 12 V WS2815 at 60 LED/m, 6.3 m per spoke.** V1's chipset, voltage and density
   are Unknown. These drive the load, the circuit count, the pixel supplies and the
   128-spoke strip cost.
2. **Watts per pixel at full white:** 0.133 / 0.24 / 0.30 (low / mid / high), from
   BTF-Lighting and Superlightingled pages. The chip datasheet is reported to be
   inconsistent, so measure one V1 spoke at full white.
3. **Load arithmetic:** 90 % supply efficiency; 60 W per segment for the switch,
   PixLite and PoE headroom; 16 A continuous per 20 A circuit (80 % rule).
4. **One power box per segment**, sitting in the daisy-chain run: inlet + outlet on
   the box, one cord per segment. Connector quantities follow from that.
5. **PixLite logic power** keeps coming from the existing 12 V supply, moved to the
   power box. If not, add a small 12 V DIN supply per box (not priced).
6. **The switch is fed from the SDR-240-48 at 54 V** through the Ultra's DC input,
   and the cheapest Ultra SKU can be told it has a 202 W supply (unconfirmed; see
   below).
7. **Ethernet joint:** plug + inline coupler + plug at each of 16 joints; RJ45
   crimped inside the node box behind a gland.
8. **Spares:** 1 switch, 1 SDR, 2 node boxes, 1 power box, 4 cable connectors and
   2 panel connectors per type, 1 GFCI.
9. **Sun shields** on all 33 boxes (16 node, 16 power, 1 brain).
10. **Expansion:** 2 cameras, 1 audio endpoint.
